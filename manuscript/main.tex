\abstract{We use 130,742 MakerDAO ETH-A debt draws as a source of observed principal sizes and timestamps for hypothetical Argentine-peso (ARS) and Turkish-lira (TRY) loans, not as a representative sample of local-currency borrowers. The 7,105 selected clean, repaid spells with exchange-rate coverage last a median 6.95 days. Matching their endpoints to published daily official reference rates gives median counterfactual net benefits of $-2.86$\% (ARS) and $-4.30$\% (TRY) under an illustrative 20\% effective annual borrowing rate and specified costs. Monthly OECD/FRED marks give $-3.00$\% and $-4.14$\% for the same spells and mechanically suppress same-month FX movement. The corresponding 12-month medians, 2.67\% and 6.59\%, impose a holding period on all eligible draws and are not observed borrowing outcomes. Borrowing-rate and position-size sensitivities, repeated draws by urn, and concentration among large positions limit generalisation. Daily published reference quotes are retrospective marks, not demonstrated executable conversion prices, especially for ARS. Conditional collateral-sale accounting is kept separate from the empirical repayment comparisons; neither liquidation frequency nor protocol solvency is estimated.}

\section{Introduction}
A borrower who owes a fixed number of local-currency units (LCU) can face a smaller USD-equivalent claim after that currency depreciates. This is a foreign-exchange accounting effect, not a measurement of consumer-price inflation. Whether it survives financing costs depends on the loan duration, position size, feasible exchange rates, and collateral outcome. A MakerDAO ETH-A draw is a DAI-denominated transaction, not evidence that anyone borrowed ARS or TRY stablecoins.

We place short reconstructed loan spells before long holding-period scenarios and distinguish voluntary repayment from nonrecourse collateral close-out. A liquidated borrower does not also repay the debt at the planned horizon. The empirical question is what observed draw sizes and timestamps imply under specified \emph{hypothetical} local-currency debt and voluntary-repayment assumptions. The collateral analysis asks only whether a terminal mark would fall below an assumed threshold. It cannot identify an actual liquidation time, auction result, or borrower return in that branch.

The contribution is an auditable mapping from public on-chain draws and official FX rates to conditional borrower-side accounting, with daily marks for short spells and robustness to borrowing rates, repeated draws, small positions, duration, and concentration. Collateral-sale and balance-sheet identities delimit the analysis rather than provide empirical solvency estimates. Prior work on lending and liquidation motivates the distinction between a collateral threshold and an executed sale \cite{Schar2021,Qin2021Liquidations,Werner2022}. Stablecoin research separates redemption and arbitrage from the value of an individual loan \cite{Lyons2023,KlagesMundt2022,GortonZhang2023}.

\section{Related research and scope}
The relevant economic mechanism is a change in the dollar value of a fixed \emph{nominal foreign-currency liability}. It need not coincide with consumer-price inflation: an ARS or TRY price index and an ARS/USD or TRY/USD exchange rate answer different questions. A borrower who retains dollar-valued proceeds while owing a fixed number of LCU units may experience an accounting reduction in dollar debt after depreciation. Whether that accounting effect can be obtained in a market depends on an accessible conversion rate, the cost of acquiring the repayment token and the time for which the loan remains open. This is why we compare selected observed loan durations with imposed longer horizons and refrain from describing an official FX mark as an executable trade.

Research on DeFi lending describes smart-contract credit and its dependence on collateral and oracle rules \cite{Schar2021,Werner2022}. Empirical liquidation research illustrates why a marked collateral threshold should not be equated with an executed auction at that price \cite{Qin2021Liquidations}. A collateral sale changes ownership of the posted asset and settles as much of the debt as its proceeds permit. Treating that sale and full voluntary repayment as simultaneous costs would misstate both borrower and issuer cash flows. Our conditional ledger makes the two outcomes mutually exclusive, but the trace and FX files used here cannot identify the prices and timing needed to estimate how often either branch would occur in a hypothetical LCU vault.

Stablecoin prices additionally depend on redemption arrangements, arbitrage access and the structure of backing \cite{Lyons2023,KlagesMundt2022,GortonZhang2023}. Those system-wide outcomes are not measured by an individual borrower's change in the dollar value of a liability. The research gap addressed here is therefore narrower than a test of a new protocol: we link public draw dates and amounts to official currency histories and explicit cost assumptions, then check whether the resulting descriptions survive changes in observational unit, duration and position size. The proposed balance-sheet identity specifies what additional data a solvency study would require.

\section{Data and observational units}
\subsection{Draw events, urns, and clean spells}
The public replication archive accompanying Chaleenutthawut et al. \cite{Chaleenutthawut2024} contains 4,466,880 successful MakerDAO Vat traces. Using the documented debt-change calculation for \texttt{frob} \cite{MakerVatDocs}, we retain 130,742 positive ETH-A draws of at least 1 DAI between January 2020 and July 2023, totalling 13.318 billion DAI, in 16,846 urn identifiers. The trace archive's exclusion of top-level calls limits the completeness of inferred debt histories. A draw is an event, not an independent borrower or necessarily a complete lifecycle; an urn is a vault identifier, not a verified person. The selection rules and archive checksum are in the accompanying documentation.

The resulting debt-change distribution is highly skewed: the median is 4,792 DAI, the mean 101,865 DAI and the maximum 150.45 million DAI. These values are independently recoverable from the tracked processed event file. The transaction hash and urn remain attached to each event for auditing. A positive \texttt{dart} can represent a further draw from an existing vault; neither the number of rows nor the number of urns identifies the number of people. In particular, a draw does not establish the time at which its associated debt was ultimately repaid.

MakerDAO is useful here because ETH-A debt draws provide recorded principal changes, UTC event times and urn identifiers under a transparent on-chain collateralised-debt mechanism. We map a DAI draw's USD-equivalent size and calendar exposure to a \emph{stipulated} LCU liability, then vary the hypothetical closing date; this holds the size and timing inputs fixed while exposing the effect of an FX path and costs. It does not follow that ARS or TRY borrowers would choose those loan sizes or entry months, face the same collateral constraints, or have access to equivalent liquidity. These data have one asset type and one historical adoption window, repeated activity within urns, skewed sizes and selected reconstructible closures. Thus neither the event distribution nor the clean-spell duration distribution represents an LCU credit market. We report size floors, urn summaries and month concentration to make these constraints visible, not to claim external validity.

We reconstruct a restricted subset of single-draw, single-repayment, non-forked clean spells. There are 7,105 spells with both monthly and daily FX coverage for each currency. Their median observed duration is 6.95 days. These are selected repaid spells and cannot estimate the duration or liquidation frequency of all vaults. Observed timestamps determine duration, while all ARS/TRY cash flows remain \emph{counterfactual}; MakerDAO did not issue an LCU loan.

\subsection{Published exchange-rate references}
$E_t$ denotes LCU per USD. The fixed-horizon scenarios retain archived OECD monthly-average series \texttt{ARGCCUSMA02STM} and \texttt{CCUSMA02TRM618N} distributed through FRED \cite{FREDARS,FREDTRY}. Using these same monthly series for a seven-day spell is problematic: entry and exit in one calendar month share one rate and gross FX benefit becomes exactly zero. For the observed-duration analysis we therefore use archived daily published references: the BCRA Communication A3500 wholesale ARS/USD reference \cite{BCRADaily} and the ECB's TRY/EUR rate divided by its USD/EUR rate to obtain TRY/USD \cite{ECBDaily}. For each UTC event date the code takes the most recent published reference \emph{calendar date no later than that date}, without interpolation or a future date. On nonpublication days it carries forward the preceding quote. The median quote-date lag is zero, its 95th percentile two days and its maximum four days across the selected endpoints. The same selected spells are also valued at arithmetic monthly means of their corresponding daily source and at the original OECD/FRED monthly marks to distinguish a change of frequency from a change of source.

These marks are retrospective reference valuations; a published quote on the same date could have appeared after the on-chain event, and the data do not show a simultaneous tradable rate. BCRA's wholesale reference is not a demonstrated retail conversion price. An Argentine rate accessible under exchange controls can differ materially from any official series. No independently vetted parallel-market series is included, so no numerical estimate is labelled executable; daily frequency fixes the zero-within-month artefact but cannot resolve market access or intraday execution.

If an executable ARS rate were $E_t^{\rm exec}=w_t E_t^{\rm official}$ for a premium $w_t>0$, the gross effect would be $1-(w_0/w_h)(E_0^{\rm official}/E_h^{\rm official})$. An unknown change in the premium can reverse even the sign of an official-rate benefit. This identity is a sensitivity argument, not an estimated parallel-rate path.

\section{Conditional borrower cash-flow model}
\subsection{Voluntary repayment}
For a draw of $P_i$ USD-equivalent DAI at origination, hypothetical initial LCU debt is $L_{i,0}=P_iE_{i,0}$. With an \emph{effective annual} LCU borrowing rate $r$, and no intermediate payments, its USD value at voluntary closing after $t$ years is
\begin{equation}
D_i(t)=P_i\frac{E_{i,0}}{E_{i,t}}(1+r)^t,\qquad
G_i(t)=P_i-D_i(t)\big|_{r=0}.
\label{eq:debt}
\end{equation}
The protocol fee $fP_i$ is paid externally in USD at opening and is \emph{not financed}. We assume a conversion friction $sP_i$ on the opening swap of hypothetical LCU proceeds into USD and $sD_i(t)$ on acquiring LCU for voluntary repayment. The illustrative gas budget $g$ is split equally: $g/2$ at opening and $g/2$ for voluntary closing. The incremental USD cash-accounting benefit relative to holding the same collateral without borrowing is
\begin{equation}
B_i^{\rm repay}(t)=P_i-fP_i-sP_i-\tfrac{g}{2}
-D_i(t)-sD_i(t)-\tfrac{g}{2}.
\label{eq:repay}
\end{equation}
Opening proceeds are assumed retained at par, with no investment return. The original collateral returns on repayment and cancels against collateral held in the no-borrowing comparator. This is neither investor profit nor welfare. Actual LCU conversions, market spread, fee funding, interest payments and taxes are unobserved. Baseline assumptions are $r=20\%$, $f=0.5\%$, $s=0.3\%$ per conversion and $g=\$40$; none is an estimated price for a deployed LCU protocol. The 20\% rate is a round, explicitly hypothetical financing-cost scenario for comparison across FX paths; it is not a historical MakerDAO stability fee, an observed peso/lira loan quote or an estimate of an equilibrium LCU rate. There is no data-supported uniquely correct baseline, so the main results show the borrowing-rate grid alongside it.

The fee timing matters: charging the opening fee on discounted debt at closure would make its dollar value depend on the later FX rate and yield a different net series. We chose an externally paid opening fee and implement that same convention in every horizon and duration check. Likewise, USD 40 refers only to two specified borrower transactions; it is not an estimate of auction gas, oracle operation or all protocol transactions. Conversion friction in the liquidation branch is charged at entry only, because this stylised borrower makes no voluntary closing swap.

\subsection{Mutually exclusive liquidation}
If the collateral is sold at time $\tau$ rather than voluntarily repaid, its USD mark before sale is $V_{i,\tau}$. A proportional auction haircut $a$ gives $S_{i,\tau}=(1-a)V_{i,\tau}$. Under an explicit nonrecourse accounting convention, recovered debt is $R_{i,\tau}=\min(S_{i,\tau},D_i(\tau))$, and a penalty $\lambda D_i(\tau)$ is collected \emph{only from surplus}:
\begin{align}
\Pi_{i,\tau}&=\min\{\lambda D_i(\tau),[S_{i,\tau}-D_i(\tau)]_+\},\\
H_{i,\tau}&=[S_{i,\tau}-D_i(\tau)]_+-\Pi_{i,\tau},&
W_{i,\tau}&=[D_i(\tau)-S_{i,\tau}]_+,\\
B_i^{\rm liq}(\tau)&=P_i-fP_i-sP_i-\tfrac{g}{2}
-V_{i,\tau}+H_{i,\tau}.
\label{eq:liquidation}
\end{align}
$H$ is cash returned to the borrower; $W$ is the unrecovered loan claim. $V-H$ is the collateral value lost relative to holding it without borrowing. The borrower neither pays $D_i(\tau)$ again in cash nor repays $D_i(h)$ at the original horizon $h$. There is no voluntary exit swap or closing gas in this branch. The auction haircut and capped penalty are distinct sale effects; a different legal recourse rule would change who bears $W$.

The detailed numerical surplus and shortfall checks are in the accompanying cash-flow note and executable tests. The available collateral shocks do not reveal $\tau$, oracle state, auction proceeds or haircut. Equation~\eqref{eq:liquidation} is an implemented \emph{conditional ledger}, not an estimated branch frequency or expected benefit. A deterministic terminal-ratio diagnostic is kept in the Appendix to show the boundary of the FX result without using it to modify the borrower medians.

\section{Empirical design and robustness}
Each draw is mapped to 3-, 6-, 12- and 24-month official-rate holding \emph{scenarios}; these are not measured MakerDAO durations. Clean spells use observed start/end timestamps and $t=\text{days}/365.25$, with daily published FX dates at the endpoints in the primary short-spell calculation. Monthly marks are a like-for-like benchmark for the same clean sample, and the borrowing rate varies from 5\% to 120\% effective annually. We report event medians, positive shares, and $\sum_i B_i/\sum_iP_i$; the last is a \emph{signed amount-weighted ratio}, not an average borrower's return. Means remain in machine-readable files but can be extremely negative for tiny draws facing fixed gas.

For dependence, we tabulate draws per urn, the chronologically first eligible draw from each urn, and an urn aggregate $100\sum_{i\in u}B_i/\sum_{i\in u}P_i$. The pooled amount-weighted result is algebraically identical for all draws and urn aggregates; this is not evidence of independence. First-draw and equal-urn summaries answer different descriptive questions. To assess concentration, we rank events by \emph{original principal size} before measuring what fraction of the full signed 12-month benefit is attributable to the largest 1, 5 and 10\% of events. Month contributions use origination month. Finally, both 12-month and clean-duration mappings are rerun with minimum draw sizes of \$1, \$100, \$1,000, \$5,000 and \$10,000.

The unit changes the question. An event median weights an urn with many draws many times; selecting the first eligible draw weights each represented urn once but discards subsequent activity. The median of within-urn amount-weighted ratios also assigns one observation to each urn while allowing its own large draws to receive greater weight. None of these three summaries identifies a representative person's payoff, because control of multiple urns by one person is unverified. The event mappings also share calendar-month FX observations. Accordingly, we report descriptive differences rather than conventional independent-event standard errors.

\section{What the comparisons identify}
The analysis contains three distinct forms of evidence. Draw timestamps, debt changes and urn identifiers come from the public MakerDAO record; the restricted clean repayment dates permit an observed spell length, subject to trace coverage and selection. Daily references and monthly official FX paths are published marks, not prices paid by these borrowers. All ARS/TRY loan balances, costs and net benefits are outcomes of a stipulated conversion and repayment rule. A positive 12-month counterfactual cannot establish that an actual borrower could remain open for 12 months, obtain official-rate ARS, or retain the original collateral throughout.

The negative clean-duration medians sharpen this distinction. At monthly frequency, a same-month opening and closing share one average and record no within-month FX movement despite charging all entry and closing costs; the daily check removes this artefact for spells spanning distinct published dates. Neither frequency demonstrates actual on-chain access to that rate. Nor does selecting successfully repaid, single-draw, non-liquidated spells establish that all ETH-A vaults had short lifetimes. An imposed long horizon is not an observed holding pattern.

The cost, size and concentration checks describe where the accounting result comes from. Raising a size floor changes both the fixed gas share of principal and the composition of months and urns; it does not merely remove numerical outliers. Similarly, the fraction of a \emph{signed} aggregate attributable to the largest draws can be high even if the median event is modest. For executable gains, large trades could move the relevant market price. The current data contain neither such trading costs nor a parallel ARS quote, so we stop at a transparent conditional comparison.

\section{Accounting boundary for protocol solvency}
The borrower benefit is not a measure of issuer capital. Borrower-posted collateral is encumbered until enforcement and is not a free asset in addition to its loan claim. Let $A_t^{\rm loan}$ be performing LCU loan claims, $A_t^{\rm recovery}$ cash after completed collateral sales with the related claim removed, $R_t$ unrestricted reserves, and $Q_t^{\rm redeem}$ and $Q_t^{\rm other}$ token redemption and other obligations. Then a schematic equity identity is
\begin{equation}
K_t=A_t^{\rm loan}+A_t^{\rm recovery}+R_t
-Q_t^{\rm redeem}-Q_t^{\rm other}.
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
A completed sale replaces the claim with recovery; $W_{i,\tau}=D_i(\tau)-R_{i,\tau}$ writes down an \emph{asset}, while token burns or settlement must separately reduce liabilities. We have no observed LCU liabilities, unrestricted reserves or auction proceeds from which to compute $K_t$. This identity only identifies data needed for a later issuer-solvency study; reserve, oracle and auction design proposals require independent testing \cite{GortonZhang2023,Arner2020,BIS2023}.

\section{Limitations and interpretation}
Published daily and monthly FX references are not necessarily executable, especially in ARS; an unknown parallel premium has no known bias direction. A same-date published reference may have arrived after the transaction. Clean spells are selected and the quote calendar is less granular than an intraday execution record. Repeated draws share urn-level risks and calendar FX shocks; no causal, confidence-interval or independent-borrower claim follows. Fixed horizons are hypothetical and cannot be inferred from clean duration. Fees, gas, conversion friction and interest rates are scenarios, not observed transaction prices for a hypothetical LCU protocol. Large positions omit market impact. The liquidation ledger assumes nonrecourse, while within-horizon collateral prices, oracles, auction discounts and settlement times are unavailable. Terminal ratios cannot identify expected cash flows. Peg stability, reserve adequacy, governance backstops, taxes and legal exchange access remain outside this dataset.

\section{Conclusion}
The most directly observed duration fact is that selected clean, repaid MakerDAO spells last a median 6.95 days. At hypothetical LCU debt terms and daily published FX reference marks, median net effects over those observed lengths are $-2.86\%$ in ARS and $-4.30\%$ in TRY at the illustrative 20\% effective rate. They remain negative at 5\% and 40\% rates; the monthly-frequency check also yields negative medians, though it misses within-month FX movement. Positive medians at 12 and 24 months apply to \emph{imposed holding periods}; amount-weighted aggregates are highly concentrated among large draws and particular months. Daily official marks are not demonstrated executable prices. The collateral ledger and issuer identity set accounting boundaries; they do not establish liquidation probabilities, a deployed protocol or its solvency.

\section*{Appendix: deterministic terminal collateral mark}
This auxiliary screen is separated from the empirical borrower comparisons because it has no asset-specific historical collateral paths or sale data. At an assumed 12-month endpoint, initial collateral worth $c_0P_i$ loses a stipulated fraction $q$, giving
\begin{equation}
{\rm CR}_{i,12}^{\rm terminal}(q,c_0)=\frac{c_0P_i(1-q)}{D_i(1)},\qquad
I_{i,12}^{\rm terminal}=\mathbf{1}\{{\rm CR}_{i,12}^{\rm terminal}<1.50\}.
\label{eq:terminal}
\end{equation}
A breach share is the percentage of \emph{event mappings} below an assumed 150\% threshold at month 12, not a probability of earlier liquidation or auction completion. The generic 0, 10, 25, 40 and 60\% declines are stipulated without shock probabilities. ETH and BTC are not distinguished because the same percentage shocks would give identical results. For a 175\% initial ratio and 25\% terminal decline, the screen flags 59.92\% of ARS and 53.41\% of TRY mappings; no expected-loss or risk-adjusted return is calculated.
