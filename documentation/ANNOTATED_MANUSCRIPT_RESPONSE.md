# Response to the annotated manuscript

The response below addresses the collaborator's three overall comments and the annotations in the supplied 11-page PDF. It refers to the current `manuscript/main.tex` and its generated `main.pdf`. All numerical statements come from tracked inputs and generated CSVs. ARS and TRY borrowing remains hypothetical; no actual parallel ARS price, LCU loan, liquidation probability or issuer balance sheet has been inferred.

## Overall structure, literature and contribution

1. **The paper was short and fragmented.** I combined the previous data, exchange-rate, cash-flow and design sections into **Data and methodology**, and combined discussion and limitations into **Discussion and limitations**. I expanded the introduction, related research, interpretation of results and conclusion. The compiled article is now 16 A4 pages. The appendix houses ancillary cost, exchange-rate-wedge and deterministic collateral screens.
2. **The aim, motivation, novelty and research question were unclear.** The introduction now opens with the economic motivation for an LCU debt denomination, states the explicit borrower-side USD cash-flow question, identifies the counterfactual and separates its contribution from the familiar FX identity. The contribution lies in testing how the interpretation changes with *observed short spells versus imposed horizons*, selected versus full draw starts, published quote timing, costs and observational unit. It does not claim to discover a new FX mechanism.
3. **Engagement with prior work was limited.** The expanded **Related research and research gap** section discusses currency mismatch and balance-sheet effects (Eichengreen, Hausmann and Panizza; Céspedes, Chang and Velasco), currency-denomination selection (Salomao and Varela), DeFi lending and MakerDAO trace data, empirical liquidation research (Qin et al.) and stablecoin liabilities. It identifies what each stream can and cannot establish for a hypothetical peso/lira borrower and explicitly states the narrower gap. Three new cited works are recorded in `manuscript/references.tex` and `manuscript/references.bib`.
4. **The opening repeated the literature section.** The introduction now states the question and design, while the literature section develops the prior findings and contrasts; repeated explanatory passages were condensed.

## Data, source selection and FX marks

5. **Why does the full draw count differ from 7,105 spells?** Table 1 and `results/selected_spells_flow.csv` show 7,947 single-draw repaid candidates, 136 excluded for nonpositive reconstructed duration, and 706 more excluded because they began before 2020. All remaining 7,105 have the required monthly and daily published FX endpoints; there is no additional FX-coverage exclusion. The 130,742 positive draws are events, not complete or independent loan lifecycles.
6. **How might missing top-level calls matter?** Data and methodology now explains that the source archive excludes these calls. An omitted debt-changing call could misclassify a purported clean repayment or its start/end time. The available files cannot quantify the error, so we do not present the selected spell durations as a survival distribution for all vaults.
7. **Compare selected spells with the wider sample.** New Table 2 and `results/sample_comparison.csv` compare count, urn count, median/P90 principal and 2020 origination share. The selected median is USD 1,500 versus USD 4,792 for all draws; 74.17% versus 56.07% began in 2020. We explain why selection changes the interpretation without claiming to identify actual LCU demand or a causal selection effect.
8. **Why do the two duration histograms coincide?** One curve now shows the common 7,105 spell durations in Figure 1; the duplicate ARS/TRY legend was removed. All figures and tables have explicit main-text references and a paragraph describing their principal pattern. Figure 3's month contributions and the machine-readable month file are named in both text and caption.
9. **Same-day reference marks could be published too late.** `analysis/daily_fx.py` now supports a strictly *previous published calendar date* at each endpoint. The new Table 6 and `results/prior_quote_sensitivity.csv` show medians of -2.88% ARS and -4.37% TRY, versus -2.86% and -4.30% with same-or-earlier dates. The result remains a published-reference mark, not an executable intraday quote.
10. **Official ARS may differ from an accessible rate.** We derive an accessible-rate multiplier $E_t^{access}=w_t E_t^{official}$ and vary the stipulated *exit-to-entry ratio* $w_h/w_0$ from 0.80 to 1.50 in the Appendix and `results/illustrative_ars_premium_sensitivity.csv`. For the selected spells, the median changes from -27.82% at 0.80 to +6.21% at 1.10. These are transparent algebraic perturbations; no unverified parallel-market path was inserted. The FX subsection explains that archived monthly series extend beyond the transaction window to supply 24-month endpoints.

## Assumptions, liquidation boundary and empirical comparisons

11. **Why these rate, USD proceeds and holding-period assumptions?** The 20% effective annual rate is described as an illustrative round benchmark rather than an observed offer; the main text shows a 5–120% rate grid. The USD-held proceeds case isolates debt denomination in one explicit USD comparator; a borrower spending LCU or holding a risky asset would require another model. Three and six months illustrate intermediate exposure, while 12 and 24 months are imposed long-horizon scenarios, not estimated MakerDAO tenors.
12. **Gas and conversion costs were not varied.** `analysis/extended_robustness.py` generates the full USD 0/20/40/80 gas by 0/0.3/1% each-way swap grid for selected daily-FX spells and full-draw 12-month cases. The Appendix presents a readable subset and the complete `results/gas_conversion_sensitivity.csv` is tracked. At 20% interest, selected medians remain -0.27% ARS and -0.50% TRY even at zero gas and zero swaps. Fee timing and which transactions incur gas remain explicit.
13. **Why a nonrecourse liquidation ledger?** The model now labels nonrecourse a *conditional modelling convention*, not a legal rule for a proposed protocol. Its role is to make repayment and sale mutually exclusive, return surplus, record unrecovered claims and prevent a later debt repayment being charged after sale. There is no probability-weighted result from these hypothetical branches; the collateral ratio screen remains in the Appendix. Actual contract recourse and auction mechanics would need separate evidence.
14. **Clarify the two empirical samples.** The empirical-comparisons subsection enumerates selected observed-duration daily-FX spells, the *same 7,105 starts* with imposed monthly-FX horizons, and all 130,742 starts with imposed horizons. Table 8 displays sample, source, endpoint, count, median and positive share. An observed-versus-imposed contrast changes the FX path as well as duration; the same-start versus all-start 12-month contrast holds horizon and FX source fixed while varying sample composition.

## Results, interpretation and limitations

15. **Observed-duration labelling and the short-versus-long contrast.** The text and tables say *observed spell length with hypothetical LCU cash flows*. At daily references the selected medians are -2.86% ARS and -4.30% TRY; all-draw 12-month medians are +2.67% and +6.59%. On the *same selected starts* imposed at 12 months, medians are +0.56% and **-4.70%**, respectively. The abstract, Results and Conclusion no longer suggest that duration alone explains the positive full-sample TRY result. Exact 3-, 6-, 12- and 24-month cases are in `results/same_sample_horizon_comparison.csv`.
16. **What produces the negative selected-spell result?** New Table 9 and `results/cashflow_decomposition.csv` reconcile gross FX, interest, upfront fee, conversion and gas as percentage-point contributions to pooled principal-weighted net benefit. For selected ARS spells these are +1.04, -0.68, -0.50, -0.60 and -0.06 points, summing to -0.80%; for TRY the net is +0.21% *weighted*, though its event median is -4.30%. The manuscript explains that pooled component totals cannot be added as medians.
17. **Positive shares, duration bands, urns and size thresholds deserve interpretation.** The Results now discuss rate-dependent positive shares; nonmonotone duration-band medians and the changing cohorts within bands; why TRY's negative urn median can coexist with a positive principal-weighted total; and how size-floor medians as well as means change. At USD 10,000 minimum the observed-duration medians are still -1.11% ARS and -1.16% TRY. The especially large TRY 12-month size shift also changes origination cohorts: 2021 accounts for 38.41% of all draws and 64.39% above USD 10,000, so it cannot be attributed to fixed gas alone.
18. **The weighted benefit is concentrated.** The manuscript explains that the largest 1% of principals contribute 72.49% ARS and 66.85% TRY of the *signed* 12-month benefit numerator, and Figure 3 shows concentration by origination month. ARS peaks in September–November 2021 (October 13.39% of the signed total), while TRY peaks in May 2021 (15.21%) with other large months in September/October. Some monthly shares are negative. This is a statement about the pooled numerator, not a representative borrower's benefit or a prediction of accessible large trades.
19. **Expand the discussion and identify the most consequential limitations.** The new discussion explains the borrower-side and issuer-side meaning of the accounting contrast, the significance of sample composition, urn weighting and concentration for evaluating an LCU protocol, and the need for observed liquidity and borrowing selection. It identifies clean-spell selection and executable FX access as the leading data limitations in one paragraph; practical costs, survival, collateral paths and settlement are addressed separately. The issuer identity's assets and liabilities are explicitly USD-valued at the *same date*, with recovered cash kept separate from reserves to avoid double counting. It remains an accounting boundary, not a solvency estimate.
20. **Conclude with contribution and future priorities.** The conclusion restates the research aim, supported empirical comparisons and their boundaries. It names transaction-time accessible FX, fuller on-chain debt histories, real LCU liquidity and borrower selection, collateral paths and auction settlements, plus issuer liability/reserve records as the data needed for the next study.
